CRNA School MMI Interview 2026 — Preparing for the Multiple Mini Interview

Part of the CRNA Career Hub — browse every related guide in one place.

Some nurse anesthesia programs interview applicants using a multiple mini interview — a circuit of short, timed stations instead of one long panel conversation. It rewards different preparation than a traditional interview. Here's how the MMI works and how to walk in ready.

The short version: An MMI is a series of short stations, each with its own prompt — an ethical scenario, a role-play, a behavioral question, or a discussion. You rotate through them on a timer, meeting a fresh evaluator each time. The design reduces the impact of one bad moment and samples your judgment across many situations.

How the MMI works

Instead of sitting with a panel for thirty minutes, you move through a circuit. At each station you get a brief prompt (often posted outside the room), a short time to read and think, then a few minutes to respond or interact with an evaluator. When time's up, you move to the next station and start fresh. Because each station is scored independently by a different person, a single stumble doesn't sink your whole interview — your next station is a clean slate.

What stations assess

MMI stations are designed to sample non-clinical competencies: ethical reasoning, communication, professionalism, teamwork, empathy, and how you handle ambiguity or pressure. Some are hypothetical scenarios ("a colleague asks you to..."), some are behavioral ("tell me about a time..."), some are role-plays with a standardized actor, and some are open discussions. The common thread is that they want to see how you think and relate, not what facts you've memorized.

How MMI differs from a panel interview

A traditional panel lets you build rapport over a longer arc; the MMI is a set of sprints. That changes your strategy: you need to read a prompt fast, organize a response quickly, deliver it clearly, and then fully reset. You can't coast on one great answer, and you can't afford to carry a rough station into the next. Both formats value authenticity — the MMI just tests it in more, shorter bursts.

Station strategy

Use the reading time. Spend the first moments identifying what the station is really asking and sketching a structure. Structure your answer. A simple frame — here's my read of the situation, here are the considerations, here's what I'd do and why, and here's how I'd account for other views — keeps you clear under the clock. For ethical scenarios, acknowledge the competing interests before committing to a defensible course. For behavioral prompts, use a real, specific ICU example with a clear outcome. For role-plays, focus on genuine communication — listen, show empathy, and respond like the professional you are.

Reset between stations. The single biggest MMI mistake is letting a weak station bleed into the next. Take a breath at each door, drop what just happened, and treat the new prompt as your first of the day.

Preparing beforehand

Practice with timed sample prompts across the station types so the format feels familiar. Build a small bank of real ICU stories you can adapt to behavioral prompts. Rehearse thinking out loud clearly and concisely. And lean on the truth that your bedside experience — ethics, difficult families, teamwork under pressure — is exactly the raw material these stations probe.

Bottom line: The MMI rewards fast, clear, authentic judgment delivered in short bursts — and forgives a single off moment. Practice the format, structure your answers, draw on real ICU experience, and reset between stations. Confirm whether your target programs use an MMI and what their specific format is, since it varies.

Frequently asked questions

What is a multiple mini interview?
A circuit of short, timed stations — each with its own prompt and evaluator — instead of one long panel interview. It samples judgment across many situations.

How is the MMI scored?
Each station is scored independently by a different evaluator, so one weak station doesn't sink the whole interview.

What's the biggest MMI mistake?
Letting a rough station bleed into the next. Reset at each door and treat every station as a clean slate.
