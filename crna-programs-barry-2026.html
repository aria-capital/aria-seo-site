Barry University, a private university in Miami Shores, Florida, runs one of the longest-established nurse anesthesia programs in the Southeast. For ICU nurses in Florida and beyond who are mapping a path to CRNA, Barry is a name that comes up often — partly because of its history and clinical footprint in South Florida, and partly because it produces a large number of graduates each year. Here's a practical overview for prospective applicants in 2026.

Verify the details before you apply. Program length, tuition, prerequisites, and admissions requirements change year to year and by cohort. Treat everything below as an orientation, not a source of truth — confirm current specifics directly with Barry University's Anesthesiology program office and the official program handbook before making decisions.

Program format and degree

Like all US nurse anesthesia programs, Barry's now culminates in a doctoral degree — the Doctor of Nurse Anesthesia Practice / DNP-level credential — in line with the national requirement that all new CRNAs graduate at the doctoral level. Programs of this type generally run about 36 months (three years) of full-time, front-loaded didactic study followed by intensive clinical residency across affiliated hospital and surgical sites. Barry has historically leveraged the dense concentration of hospitals in the Miami metro area for clinical placements.

Typical prerequisites

Nurse anesthesia admissions requirements are fairly standardized across programs. For Barry, expect the core elements common to competitive CRNA schools:

  • An unencumbered RN license and a BSN (or equivalent) from an accredited program.
  • A minimum of one year — realistically more — of recent, full-time critical care experience in an adult ICU where you manage vasoactive drips, ventilators, and invasive lines.
  • A competitive GPA, with science and graduate-level coursework weighted heavily; many admitted applicants exceed a 3.5.
  • GRE requirements that vary — some programs have dropped it, others still require or recommend it, so confirm the current cycle's policy.
  • CCRN certification, strong references, a personal statement, and a formal interview.
  • Shadowing a CRNA and, increasingly, graduate-level chemistry or pathophysiology depending on the year's requirements.

Admissions competitiveness

CRNA admission is competitive everywhere, and Barry is no exception. The differentiators that move applications are consistent across programs: the quality and acuity of your ICU experience (a high-acuity unit — CVICU, medical/surgical ICU, or trauma — reads stronger than a step-down), CCRN certification, upward GPA trends, and a compelling interview. Because Barry's cohorts are relatively large, it accepts more students than some boutique programs, but the applicant pool is deep and the bar remains high.

Cost and financing

Barry is a private university, and private CRNA program tuition typically lands in the six figures across the full program once fees, living costs, and the near-impossibility of working during school are accounted for. That reality is worth planning around: most nurse anesthesia students rely on a combination of federal graduate loans and savings. The upside is the payoff — CRNAs are among the highest-earning advanced practice nurses, which is why the return on the investment tends to be strong despite the sticker price.
