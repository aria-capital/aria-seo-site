What the Compact License Is

The NLC is an agreement between participating states that recognizes each other's nursing licenses. If your primary state of residence is a compact state, you automatically have a multistate license — meaning you can work in any other compact state without applying for a separate license there. You still follow each state's nursing practice act when you work there, but you do not need to apply, pay fees, or wait for approval in each state.

For travel nurses, this is transformative. Without a compact license, taking an assignment in a new state means 6-12 weeks of licensing paperwork, endorsement fees of $50-$200, and delays that can cost you contracts. With a compact license, you can accept contracts in most of the country within days.

How to Get a Compact License

You do not apply for a "compact license" separately — your existing license becomes multistate automatically if your primary state of residence is a compact state. Here is how it works:

  1. Your home state must be a compact state. The compact is based on where you live, not where you currently hold a license. If you live in Texas and have an active Texas license, you have a multistate license.
  2. You must maintain your primary residence in a compact state. If you move to California (non-compact), your multistate privilege ends and you need only a California license.
  3. Your home state license must be active and in good standing. Expired, inactive, or disciplined licenses lose multistate privileges.
  4. You can practice in any compact state under your home state license, following that state's practice laws.

If You Currently Live in a Non-Compact State

This is the most common question from nurses who want to travel. If you live in California, New York, or another non-compact state, you cannot get a compact license until you change your primary state of residence to a compact state. Some travel nurses strategically establish residency in a compact state — typically Texas, Florida, or a nearby compact state — to gain multistate privileges.

Endorsement: How to Get Licensed in Non-Compact States

When you need to work in a non-compact state (or need primary licensure there), you apply for endorsement — transferring your active RN license to that state. General process:

  1. Verify you meet the new state's requirements (some states require additional coursework, live hours, or exams)
  2. Submit application to the new state's board of nursing with the endorsement fee ($50-$200)
  3. Request NURSYS verification from your home state (most states use NURSYS for electronic verification)
  4. Wait for processing — ranges from 2 weeks (Texas) to 6 months (California)
  5. Some states issue temporary practice permits that allow you to work while the full license processes

NURSYS: The Endorsement Shortcut

NURSYS is the online nurse license verification system operated by NCSBN. Most compact and many non-compact states participate. When you apply for endorsement, submitting through NURSYS can reduce processing time significantly because verification is electronic rather than paper-based. Check nursys.com to see if your home state and target state both participate before starting your endorsement.
