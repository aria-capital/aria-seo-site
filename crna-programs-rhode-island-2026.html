CRNA Programs in Rhode Island

Part of the CRNA Career Hub — browse every related guide in one place.

Rhode Island offers a doctoral nurse anesthesia pathway through Rhode Island College in partnership with the long-running St. Joseph Hospital School of Nurse Anesthesia. For a small state, that is a real in-state opportunity — but a single, competitive one, so plan to apply regionally as well.

The landscape: Rhode Island's CRNA pathway runs through Rhode Island College's Zvart Onanian School of Nursing, whose BSN–DNP Nurse Anesthesia specialization is delivered in collaboration with the historic St. Joseph Hospital School of Nurse Anesthesia (SJHSNA). It is a doctoral program (DNP) and the state's primary in-state CRNA route. With one main pathway serving all of Rhode Island, in-state applicants should plan to apply across New England as well.

Rhode Island College's Zvart Onanian School of Nursing awards the BSN–DNP with a Nurse Anesthesia specialization, building on the clinical training tradition of the St. Joseph Hospital School of Nurse Anesthesia — one of the older hospital-based nurse anesthesia schools in the region. The program is delivered at the doctoral level, in line with the national requirement that all entry-level CRNAs now graduate with a DNP or DNAP. As the state's primary in-state pathway, it is the natural first target for Rhode Island nurses.

Clinical Training and Focus

The partnership pairs Rhode Island College's graduate nursing coursework with hospital-based anesthesia training through St. Joseph Hospital and affiliated clinical sites. Students move from foundational sciences and simulation into progressively independent clinical rotations across the surgical specialties. For applicants in southern New England, the Providence location keeps clinical placements close to home, an advantage when balancing a demanding program with family and finances.

Admission Requirements

The program follows the national CRNA template. You will need a BSN (or equivalent qualifying nursing degree) with a competitive GPA, a minimum of one year of full-time adult critical-care experience, an unencumbered RN license, and current BLS and ACLS certification. The ICU requirement is non-negotiable everywhere, and competitive applicants typically bring more than the minimum — high-acuity experience and the CCRN credential strengthen a file; see the CCRN guide. For the complete checklist, review the general CRNA school requirements.

One in-state pathway means you should apply widely. A single established program is a real opportunity, but treating it as your only target is risky. Build a regional list that includes nearby New England programs — Massachusetts and Connecticut — to improve your odds considerably.

Application Strategy for Rhode Island

Because Rhode Island runs essentially one in-state pathway, your plan should be regional from day one. Apply to the Rhode Island College / SJHSNA program within its April 1 – July 1 window, but build a list that also includes Massachusetts and Connecticut to give yourself realistic odds across a competitive applicant pool. Strengthen your file with the required ICU year (ideally more), the CCRN, and a sharp personal statement. For realistic expectations, see CRNA acceptance rates.

Bottom Line

Rhode Island's CRNA pathway runs through Rhode Island College's BSN–DNP Nurse Anesthesia specialization, delivered with the St. Joseph Hospital School of Nurse Anesthesia in Providence — a genuine doctoral opportunity with a defined April–July application window. With a single in-state pathway, Rhode Island applicants should apply regionally across New England to give themselves a fair shot.
